Borrowing is far from even

In 2024-25 the average Queenslander borrowed 7.7 items from their council library. Gold Coast residents borrowed 12.3 each, while some remote councils borrowed fewer than one. Councils with no published figures are shown in grey.

Section Synthesis: State circulation figures are skewed by strong borrowing performance in South East Queensland, while over 85% of Queensland councils log circulation rates well below the state average.

Busy doors, big and small

Visits tell a different story from loans. Brisbane recorded 5.4 million visits and the Gold Coast 2 million. Across Australia, in-person visits fell during the pandemic and have recovered to about 3.2 per person, while website visits have dropped.

Section Synthesis: While digital access surged during lockdowns, physical branches remain the dominant operational footprint for public libraries, driven overwhelmingly by high-density coastal population centers.

Do the measures move together?

Larger councils generally borrow more, but smaller rural councils vary widely. Below, each line is one council. The ten highest-borrowing councils are in teal and all the others are pale grey. Hover over a teal dot to highlight one council, and follow its line across to see whether it also ranks high on membership, visits and advantage.

Section Synthesis: Parallel metrics confirm that structural capacity (independent vs. rural) impacts baseline engagement, yet individual top-performing councils achieve high circulation through deep engagement rather than raw membership size.

What libraries offer, and how that is changing

Council programs lean heavily towards young children. Early childhood programs are about 31% of all sessions Australia-wide but draw about 51% of everyone who attends. Compared with before COVID, Australians now borrow about 18% more items each, while website visits are down.

Section Synthesis: Public library programming delivers disproportionately high community value through early childhood literacy sessions, reinforcing the library's evolving role as a physical social infrastructure hub.

Who pays for the library?

Most of the money comes from local councils, with State Library of Queensland grants adding a smaller amount per resident. The gap between the two varies a lot between councils.

Section Synthesis: Municipal rate revenues dominate library operations, resulting in substantial funding disparities across council boundaries based on local tax base capacities.
