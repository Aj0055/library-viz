Australian libraries: where they are and who they reach

More than 1,700 public library outlets serve Australians, from big city branches to small outback libraries. Do people use them equally everywhere? We start with the whole country, then take a closer look at Queensland.

1Australia at a glance

Each state and territory runs its own library network, so the figures are not perfectly comparable. Even so, the national picture is clear: people borrow very different amounts depending on where they live.

Taking a closer look at Queensland

Queensland borrows the most per person of any state, and it publishes figures for each of its local councils. That lets us see how use differs inside one state, from the south-east coast to the outback.

2Borrowing is far from even

In 2024-25 the average Queenslander borrowed 7.7 items from their council library. Gold Coast residents borrowed 12.3 each, while some remote councils borrowed fewer than one. Councils with no published figures are shown in grey.

Nine councils borrow fewer than two items per resident, while only 16 of the 62 reach six or more.

3Busy doors, big and small

Visits tell a different story from loans. Brisbane recorded 5.4 million visits and the Gold Coast 2 million. Across Australia, in-person visits fell during the pandemic and have recovered to about 3.2 per person, while website visits have dropped.

Brisbane alone accounts for 32% of the visits recorded, about as much as the next four councils combined.

5Do the measures move together?

Larger councils generally borrow more, but smaller rural councils vary widely. Below, each line is one council. The ten highest-borrowing councils are in teal and all the others are pale grey. Hover over a teal dot to highlight one council, and follow its line across to see whether it also ranks high on membership, visits and advantage.

The top 10 borrowers span SEIFA deciles 3 to 9, and include one small rural council, Winton, with about 1,100 residents.

6What libraries offer, and how that is changing

Council programs lean heavily towards young children. Early childhood programs are about 31% of all sessions Australia-wide but draw about 51% of everyone who attends. Compared with before COVID, Australians now borrow about 18% more items each, while website visits are down.

Nationally, libraries ran a record 409,000 programs in 2023-24 for more than 7 million attendances.

7Who pays for the library?

Most of the money comes from local councils, with State Library of Queensland grants adding a smaller amount per resident. The gap between the two varies a lot between councils.
